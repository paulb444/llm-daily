# LLM Industry Daily — November 25, 2026


## ⚡ What Matters Today

- **Nvidia adds an export-control committee and starts a compliance-chief search.** After reinstating Huang on November 22, the board formed a National Security and Export Control Committee "comprised solely of independent directors" ([Nvidia IR](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Board-Reinstates-Jensen-Huang-as-President-CEO-and-Board-Director/)). **Take:** Permanent compliance machinery suggests the board expects export scrutiny to persist despite White House backing.
- **US adds Megaspeed and its Thai and Malaysian subsidiaries to the Entity List.** Commerce's list restricts export, reexport and in-country transfers; Megaspeed says it complies with US export controls ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/us-govt-adds-singapores-megaspeed-and-subsidiaries-to-export-block-list/)). **Take:** Enforcement is moving to Southeast Asian intermediaries, raising diligence costs for regional neoclouds buying Nvidia hardware.
- **TVA reports $1.7 billion in aggregate payments from one unnamed data center customer.** Per a Nov. 12 SEC filing, the payments fall under the new tariff for loads above 5 MW; the period is not stated ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/tva-announces-17b-data-center-deal-under-new-large-load-tariff/875630/)). Capacity charges run about $1.5 million per MW above 5 MW. **Take:** Up-front charges make data centers carry stranded-cost risk, unlike West Virginia's household surcharge below.
- **Applied Digital brings its first 25MW Polaris Forge 2 hall online and signs a $5.4bn hyperscaler deal.** The Harwood, North Dakota hall is ready for service; the 18-year agreement is $5.4bn base-term revenue, rising to $12.7bn with all renewals (the $12.7bn includes the $5.4bn) ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/applied-digital-brings-online-25mw-data-hall-at-polaris-forge-2-data-center-in-north-dakota/)). **Take:** Execution, not land, is becoming the scarce asset.
- **Vantage drops its 500MW Trident project after Aurora passes a data center moratorium.** The Aurora, Colorado site was planned at 860,000 sq ft and 500MW (334MW IT); the moratorium runs through September 2027 or until further council action ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/vantage-drops-plans-for-trident-data-center-after-colorados-aurora-city-passes-moratorium/)). **Take:** Moratoria now kill projects at site selection rather than just slowing them.
